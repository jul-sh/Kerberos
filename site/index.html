01 Reading

Lineage. Kerberos is a Dutch-baroque serif in the Kis–Janson line. Miklós Tótfalusi Kis cut the first of these types in Amsterdam around 1685, and they were sold for centuries as Janson. The style is compact, sturdy and economical, and common in book typography.

Features. It has the typical Kis–Janson details: the drooping ball ear of the g, the curved bars of A and B, the straight angled leg of R, an even colour on the page and a high-contrast Bold. The italic is a separate, narrower design. There are four axes: weight from Light to Bold, optical size from 6 to 72 point, grade and lowercase height. Small caps, the full set of f-ligatures, and Latin, Greek and Cyrillic are included.

Figures. Oldstyle figures are the default, so a date like 14 March 1879 matches the lowercase. Lining figures are available for tables and headings: 1,250 COPIES IN 2026.

Heritage. Kerberos is based on Alegreya by Juan Pablo del Peral (Huerta Tipográfica) and Crimson Pro by Jacques Le Bailly and Sebastian Kosch. It is released under the SIL Open Font License. All earlier Kis–Janson types are commercial.
