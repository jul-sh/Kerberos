05 Reading

Lineage. Kerberos belongs to the Kis–Janson–Ehrhardt line. This Dutch-baroque genre is a cornerstone of book typography. Miklós Tótfalusi Kis cut the first types in Amsterdam around 1685. They were sold for centuries as Janson, revived by Monotype as Ehrhardt in 1938 and reworked by Matthew Butterick as Equity in 2011. The type is compact, sturdy and economical on the page, a face for setting books.

Features. From Ehrhardt come the drooping ball ear of the g, the gently curved bars of A and B and the straight angled leg of R. From Equity come the even colour and the contrasted Bold. Weight runs from Light 300 to Bold 700, and the italic is a separate, narrower hand. Optical size runs from 8 to 72 point and follows the text size: small text is wider, larger on the body and lower in contrast; display sizes are tighter, with finer hairlines. Grade, from −50 to +100, changes the contrast of the thin strokes without moving a single line; at −50 the hairlines have Ehrhardt's contrast. Small caps, the full set of f-ligatures (fi, fl, ff, ffi, ffl, fj, fb, fh, fk, ft) and Latin, Greek and Cyrillic support complete the family.

Figures. Oldstyle figures are the default, so a date like 14 March 1879 sits within the lowercase. Lining figures are there for tables and headings: 1,250 COPIES IN 2026.

Heritage. Kerberos is drawn on Alegreya by Juan Pablo del Peral (Huerta Tipográfica) and released under the SIL Open Font License. Every earlier member of the line is commercial.
